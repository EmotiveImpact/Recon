# Why Recon exists, and the thinking behind it

Written 13 October 2026 from the conversation with Edem that started it. Update this file whenever a decision changes.

## Where it came from

Recon started in SALEANE's "Elevator pitch" thread. SALEANE is an all-in-one app for salons, spas and studios (£59 a month per location). Working out its pitch led to three questions in turn:

1. **How big is the market?** About 325,000 businesses with staff across the UK and US that SALEANE could serve: beauty and wellness about 190,000, physio, chiro and osteo about 89,000, barbers and tattoo about 31,000, aesthetics and med spas about 16,600. Sources and the five-year projection are on the market page: https://claude.ai/artifact/PDMj4G6VyfxMKK85NYbp64
2. **How many could we realistically bring on?** With a strong campaign and a clear problem solved, about 150 UK locations in the first six months (60 floor, 300 stretch), then the US from month six. Studios rarely switch mid-year, and free move-ins take hands-on time.
3. **How do we reach them, for free?** That is what Recon does.

Edem then asked for it to live in its own repository and to serve **all** his projects, not just SALEANE. That is why target lists are files and nothing about salons is built into the code.

## Decisions so far

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Open data only: Overture Maps, OpenStreetMap, Companies House | Free, legal to reuse, and covers every business type and country we care about. |
| Never scrape Google Maps, booking sites (Fresha, Treatwell, Booksy and so on) or the search pages of professional registers (HCPC, GOsC, GCC) | Their terms forbid it, and harvesting from registers is a data protection risk. Overture gives much the same data openly. |
| Official bulk downloads that a government publishes for reuse are allowed, including state licence files (Edem, 13 October 2026) | Edem asked for every open list. A file a state or regulator offers as a download (Texas TDLR, NYC buildings licences, Virginia DPOR, California CSLB and the like) is published for reuse, unlike a search page. Each source's terms are recorded in its `licence` column and honoured: Washington's ban on marketing to lists of individuals makes its sole traders post only. |
| One master database of open registers, UK and US, any kind of organisation (13 October 2026) | Edem wants a single list to pick targets from: companies, charities, students' unions, trade unions, schools, care services, clubs and more. One loader per register in `recon/sources/`, a common set of columns, and every row says which register it came from, under what licence and when. See MASTER.md. |
| "can email" in the master database is decided per register and legal form | UK companies, LLPs, CIOs, charitable companies, CICs, registered societies and public bodies yes; trusts, unincorporated charities and associations (most trade unions), partnerships, sole traders and childminders post only. US yes under CAN-SPAM, except Washington's individuals. When one register shows an organisation is a company, the merged organisation may be emailed. |
| Duplicates across registers are joined by company number, name and postcode, a shared business email or a shared own website | A key shared by more than five records (an accountant's inbox, a chain's website) joins nothing, and the same name at one postcode joins at most fifty. The joining runs in DuckDB because Python ran out of memory at three million records. |
| Left out of the master database | The ICO register (its emails are named people and its licence excludes personal data), the business rates valuation list (rating purposes only), the Scottish charity register (bans direct marketing), anything non-commercial, scraped or breached. Web Data Commons waits on Edem because it states no licence. |
| Recon's own searches feed the master database (13 October 2026) | The registers rarely give emails; Recon's runs read them from businesses' own websites. The newest finished list per target is loaded as the source `recon-runs`, so the website search over the master database shows those contacts. First used for UK plumbers. |
| Contact-rich open lists in the master database (13 October 2026) | Registers rarely give emails, so Overture, People Data Labs, Web Data Commons, Wikidata, Gender Pay Gap, Modern Slavery, SBA PPP and Companies House owners and accounts were added. UK emails went from about 152,000 organisations to 871,000. Owners and accounts are "enrich only": kept only when they join a company found elsewhere. Grouping now works on record numbers and hashed keys, and merging runs one source and one slice of groups at a time, so 54 million entries fit the cloud workspace's disk. |
| ~~Only look up emails for businesses we may email~~ Show every contact detail found, for every business (Edem decided, 13 October 2026) | Edem wants to see it all to make the decisions. Every email (personal ones labelled), every phone (mobiles told apart from landlines) and every social link is kept. `contact_rule` still decides who may be emailed, named people are still only worked out for companies, and nothing is sent. `--can-email-only` brings back the old, leaner behaviour. The lists stay private in `data/` and `out/`. |
| When unsure, the answer is "post only" | A business we could not match to Companies House might be a sole trader. Post to businesses is always allowed, so this never puts us in the wrong. |
| The main email is one the business publishes on its own website | Those are addresses the business chose to make public for enquiries. |
| A named director's address may be worked out, for companies we may email only (decided 13 October 2026) | Emails to the owner get read; shared inboxes often don't. It is what Hunter and others do. It is kept in its own columns (`contact_*`), with a score and reason, never mixed up with published addresses. See RULES.md. |
| Build the email finders' free techniques ourselves rather than add their tools | ColdReach, MailScout and theHarvester all do some of this, but each brings dependencies and a young or quiet codebase. The ideas (patterns, catch-all detection, mail record checks) are a few hundred lines of standard library. |
| Reacher stays optional and outside Recon | It is AGPL-licensed. Recon only talks to it over the network if you run it, so its licence never touches Recon's code. Recon also has its own simpler mailbox check. |
| Hunter only as a plug-in, off by default | It costs money beyond a small free allowance. It only runs with `--hunter` and a key Edem supplies, and only for businesses whose own website gave nothing to go on. |
| Use the emails, phones and social links Overture lists (decided 13 October 2026) | Overture's 2026-09-23.1 release added `emails` and `socials`. In a Bath test of 216 places: 114 listed an email (58 of them personal ones), 210 a phone (55 mobile only) and 186 a social link. Listed emails score 75, the same as OpenStreetMap; an address also found on the business's own site is upgraded to 90. |
| ~~Near-match businesses to Companies House~~ Match companies on rare shared words and the distance to the registered office (13 October 2026, after checking real data) | The first matcher, checked by hand against Brighton and Bath, made two kinds of mistake. Generic words: seven Brighton salons were given to HAIR (BRIGHTON) LIMITED because they shared only "hair" and the town, and two Bath salons to BATH SPA NAILS LTD. Same name, other city: "Avenue Beauty" in Brighton was matched to a company in Liverpool, "Muse Hair" to one in Edinburgh, which marks sole traders "can email". Now every word counts by how rare it is among company names in the trade (Splink's idea), and trade, town, street and area words never count as rare. The distance to the registered office comes from GeoNames' free postcode file: at the same postcode or street a fair likeness with a rare word is enough; within 5 km a close one; within 40 km (an accountant in the next town) only the very same name, and one at most two companies have; further away, no match. Different trades never match ("Luxe Nails", LUXE HAIR LTD), nor do different numbers ("Studio 7", STUDIO 8 LTD). When two companies fit equally, the one with the very same name wins, else neither. When one company would take several differently named places, only the strongest match at its own address keeps it. Result: Brighton 126 "can email" of 918 (the old matcher said 168), Bath 40 of 272 (old 53); every one was checked by eye, with 4 uncertain. A random sample of 80 matches across the UK had 78 right and 2 doubtful, both "close name" matches. After the category fix below: Brighton 151 of 1,074 and Bath 50 of 319, the 39 new or changed matches again checked by eye; a further UK sample of 50 matches from the new categories and joined names had 1 wrong (since fixed) and 2 doubtful. |
| Trade words come from data, not from the code (13 October 2026) | Edem wants Recon for any kind of business, not just salons. Words that describe a trade ("hair", "plumbing", "dental") are kept in `recon/trades.json`, grouped by trade, for about 30 trades, and a target adds its own with `"trade_words"`. The words of a target's own categories and tags count too, as do words found in many of a run's names. Duplicate joining and company matching both use the same list (`recon/words.py`). In company matching, a trade word from a trade the target is not about counts as an ordinary word: in a run of salons "Baker" in APRIL BAKER LTD is a surname. A salon called "April", run by someone else, was once given to that company, because "Baker" counted as a trade and also as a local street name (a Baker Street is in the same district). Street and area names elsewhere in the district now only excuse words both names share. |
| Read open registers as extra places, starting with the CQC directory (13 October 2026) | Regulators publish every licensed business of a kind under the Open Government Licence. The Care Quality Commission's directory (57,000 dentists, GPs, clinics and care homes in England) gives the phone, website and the operator's legal name. In a Bath dentists test it joined 36 of 54 map listings, added 5 missing ones, and named the operating company for 21, which settles the Companies House match. Any open CSV can be added with its columns named. Operators are kept only when they are organisations, never a practitioner's own name. Council "special treatment" licence lists (massage, nails, tattoo) can be added the same way where a council publishes one openly. |
| Use the full public free-mail and throwaway lists (13 October 2026) | Downloaded to `data/` when first needed: free-email-domains (MIT, 32,000 providers) and disposable-email-domains (CC0). In Brighton this relabelled 4 of 911 addresses as personal (btconnect.com, talk21.com and the like): small, but free. |
| Leave People Data Labs' free company list out for now (13 October 2026) | It is 2.4 GB (3.3 million UK companies, CC BY 4.0) and could fill missing websites. Measured: it found a website for 20 of 293 Brighton businesses without one, and 3 of 69 in Bath, and where both had one it agreed about 9 times in 10 ("The Cutting Room" in Bath was given a salon in Westbury). Too little for its size; worth trying later with a check that the suggested site really mentions the business. |
| No Common Crawl fallback for websites that fail (13 October 2026) | Of 570 Brighton websites, 207 could not be opened: 152 because the domain no longer exists, 51 refused or had no page, 4 timed out. An archive cannot help with a dead domain (its email bounces), and the rest are few. Common Crawl's index is also blocked from the cloud workspace. If it is ever added, it may only fill websites, phones and social links: Common Crawl's terms of use forbid harvesting personal data from its pages and using it for spam, so emails keep coming only from the businesses' own sites, read live. |
| Cap DuckDB's memory at 2 GB (13 October 2026) | Two OpenStreetMap reads at once were killed for memory. With the cap a town's read peaks at 2.7 GB and the whole UK finishes. |
| Read the business details websites state for search engines | Many salon sites carry schema.org JSON-LD or microdata with their email, phone and social links, even when the page text doesn't. |
| Use the site map when a homepage links to no contact page | Menus drawn by JavaScript hide links from a simple reader; the site map still lists the pages. It costs one extra request. |
| Stricter address checks | Addresses with broken shapes, placeholders (name@, your@) or throwaway domains are dropped. The full public throwaway list can be added as `data/disposable_domains.txt`. |
| A mobile-only business is flagged (`mobile_only`) | A studio reached only on a mobile is often one person working alone, a hint it may be a sole trader even when it looks like a company. |
| Overture's category column changed in 2026-09-23.1 | `categories.primary/alternate` became `taxonomy.primary/alternates`. Recon checks which columns a release has, so older and newer releases both work. Permanently closed places are left out. |
| Check a target's categories against each release (13 October 2026) | The same release renamed categories too, and a renamed one silently finds nothing. SALEANE's list had 13 dead names; "spas" alone hid about 14,000 UK spas and beauty rooms now called "spa", and "chiropractor", "osteopath" and "skin_care" became "chiropractic", "osteopathic_medicine" and "skin_care_and_makeup". The list now uses the new names (each sampled by hand first), and the places step names any category that found nothing. "personal_or_beauty_service" (7,000 UK places) is left out: a sample was half salons, half key cutters, cleaners and engravers. |
| A joined business keeps every name its sources give (13 October 2026) | Records joined by a shared phone or website can carry different names: a Brighton tattoo shop is "Magic Tattoo" to two sources and "Lazerase Laser Tattoo Removal", the removal service it runs, to a third. The name most sources give now leads, the rest go in `other_names`, and the company match tries each name; when another name matches, that name leads the row instead. Before this, the shop lost its match to MAGIC TATTOO BRIGHTON LTD. |
| Read OpenStreetMap from the country's daily extract, not Overpass (13 October 2026) | The public Overpass server is blocked from the cloud workspace and slow and busy elsewhere. Recon now downloads the country's `.osm.pbf` extract (Geofabrik, or the openstreetmap.fr mirror when Geofabrik fails; 2.6 GB for the UK), keeps it for a week in `data/osm/`, and reads it with DuckDB's spatial extension. The whole UK takes about 85 seconds and gives full tags, addresses included. A shop drawn as a building outline is placed at the average of its corners. `--osm-overpass` keeps the old route. |
| Join duplicates by place and name, not exact name (13 October 2026) | In Brighton, 97 of 289 OpenStreetMap-only rows were the same shop as an Overture row a few metres away under a slightly different name ("Taper Barbers", "Taper Barber"; "Max Oliver", "MaxOliver. Brighton"), which would have meant duplicate rows and duplicate emails. Records within 120 metres are one business when their names are alike (word order, spacing, plurals, one name a shorter form of the other), or they share a phone number or website page. Trade words (hair, nails, barbers, tattoo) and town and street words do not count as the name, and names in different trades ("Luxe Nails", "Luxe Hair") are never joined. Each of the 120 joined groups in Brighton was checked by hand. Records with no position still join on name and postcode. |
| Mailbox checks are a separate step that can run elsewhere | They need port 25, which this cloud workspace and most home connections block. `recon verify --smtp-from` can run later on a small rented server. |
| Be polite when crawling: robots.txt, a few pages per site, one page a second per site, an honest user agent | We are guests on small businesses' websites. |
| Standard library only, apart from DuckDB for Overture | Easy to run anywhere, nothing to break. |
| Keep it in-house, not a product for sale (for now) | Apollo, Cognism, Hunter, Outscraper and Clay already sell this. Prove it on SALEANE first; a sector-specific version (for example, helping beauty suppliers find studios) could be looked at later with evidence. |
| Recon finds people; it does not send | Sending needs a separate domain, a suppression list and an unsubscribe flow. That is a deliberate next stage, see NEXT.md. |
| Publish big master tables only in parts of about 200 MB (14 October 2026) | The shared project folder never passed on any file over about 500 MB: `master.parquet` (5.5 GB), `organisations.parquet`, `uk/england.parquet` (568 MB) and three big source files sat "waiting to upload" for over an hour, held in the workspace's memory (13 GB of 15), until a rebuild ran out of memory. Files up to about 150 MB went through. So every table over 200 MB is also written as `<name>/part-N.parquet` and only the parts are published. `RECON_MEMORY` lowers DuckDB's memory cap when the workspace is short. |
| The repository is public, the data never is | `.gitignore` blocks `data/`, `out/` and every CSV, zip and parquet file. |
| In the UK only reserved company words make a name "can email" (15 October 2026) | When a register gives no legal form, the master database read the name. It counted "& Co", "Company", "LP", "Inc" and "PC" as company words for UK businesses too, which made about 44,000 UK organisations "can email" (4,800 with an email address) on evidence such as "Raymond Barker & Co" or "The Sign and Workwear Company". Those are often partnerships or sole traders, a limited partnership is not a corporate body in England and Wales, and the Companies House matcher already treats limited partnerships as post only. UK names now need Ltd, Limited, PLC, LLP, CIC, CIO or SCIO; US names keep the longer list. |
| British rules decide for a British organisation (15 October 2026) | Some UK organisations were joined to a US listing of the same business (for example an Overture US place with the same website). The US record is "can email" under CAN-SPAM, and one "can email" was enough for the whole organisation, so about 5,500 UK organisations were emailable only because of a US record. Now, when any record of an organisation is British, only British records can make it "can email". |

## Open-source projects we learn from

Recon stays standard library only, so it takes ideas, not code. Where an idea came from a project with a permissive licence, a comment at that spot names it.

- Ideas used (permissive licences): Splink and uk_address_matcher (MIT) for near-match linking; extruct (BSD) for structured data; trafilatura and courlan (Apache 2.0) for finding pages through site maps; phonenumbers (Apache 2.0) for UK number rules; email-validator (Unlicense) for address checks; MailScout and ColdReach (MIT) for patterns and catch-all checks; disposable-email-domains (CC0) for throwaway domains.
- Read for ideas only, never copy from: Reacher (AGPL), theHarvester (GPL), EmailFinder (GPL), fuzzywuzzy (GPL) and nameparser (LGPL). Copying their code would put Recon under their licence terms.
- Not to be used at all: openleads (forbids commercial use) and local-business-lead-finder (custom licence).
- For sending later, listmonk (AGPL) is fine to run as a separate program, never merged into Recon.

## How each step works, briefly

- **Places.** Overture is queried with DuckDB straight from its public S3 bucket (anonymously, so stray AWS keys on a computer don't get it refused), filtered by country box, country code, category and a confidence of at least 0.5. OpenStreetMap is read from the country's downloaded extract with DuckDB (see the decisions above). Open registers the target names are read too, each row placed by its postcode. Duplicates, across and within sources, are records within 120 metres with alike names or a shared phone or website page, or with the same name and postcode; Overture's record wins and the others fill gaps.
- **Companies.** The Companies House file is filtered to active companies with the target's trade (SIC) codes, plus any company an open register names as a business's operator. Each business is compared with companies of the same name anywhere and companies sharing a word with it in its postcode district, and matched on rare shared words and the distance to the registered office (see the decisions above). When unsure, there is no match and the business stays "post only". `company_match` says how each match was made, for example "close name (0.93), same postcode" or "same name, registered 14 km away".
- **Rules.** See RULES.md.
- **People.** For companies we may email that have their own website, the Companies House API (free, needs a key) gives the active directors. One goes on the row.
- **Emails.** The homepage, then up to three same-site pages (contact pages first, then about and team pages; `/contact` if none is linked). Every address is kept in the cache, including ones hidden from robots by Cloudflare or written as "name [at] domain [dot] com". The best address for the row is one on the business's own domain, then a shared inbox name such as info@ or bookings@.
- **Patterns.** Personal addresses on the site are matched against the directors' names to learn the pattern (sarah@, sarah.jones@, sjones@ and so on). If no name matches, the shape of an address is a weaker hint (j.smith@ suggests f.last). With nothing at all, Recon borrows the pattern seen most often on other businesses' sites in the same run, and scores the guess low.
- **Confidence.** Every address gets a score from 0 to 100 and a sentence saying why. Published on its own site starts at 90; a director's address made from a matched pattern at 70; a borrowed guess at 30. A confirmed mailbox lifts it to 90 or more; a domain that cannot take mail, or a mailbox the server says does not exist, drops it to 0. 70 or more counts as ready.
- **Checks.** First, does the domain take email at all (its mail records; Recon asks DNS itself, falling back to DNS over HTTPS where ordinary DNS is blocked). Then, if Reacher is running or `--smtp-from` is given, does the mailbox exist. Recon asks about a made-up address first so a server that accepts everything ("catch-all") is not taken as proof, tries the next likeliest patterns for a guessed person, and waits at least ten seconds between conversations with Google or Microsoft.

## The first real runs (13 October 2026)

All run in the cloud workspace, which reaches Overture, the OpenStreetMap download mirror, Companies House, GeoNames, the CQC and ordinary websites, but not port 25 (mailbox checks) or the public Overpass server.

- **Whole UK, SALEANE's target list** (with the categories renamed to Overture's new names). Overture gave 162,751 places and OpenStreetMap 47,293; once joined, 174,533 businesses (28,125 found in both, and 17,195 listed under more than one name). 20,702 matched a company and may be emailed; 153,831 are post only. By sector, can email / post only: beauty and wellness 13,627 / 107,517; barbers and tattoo 3,798 / 24,033; body and health 1,488 / 8,651; aesthetics 1,288 / 5,224; pet grooming 501 / 8,406. 53% (93,364) list a website, 46% (80,749) an email, 86% a phone and 79% a social link. The places step took 11 minutes, the companies step 3. (The first run, before the category fix, found 153,804 businesses and 17,957 to email.)
- **Brighton, websites read.** Of 570 websites, 273 gave an email, 89 had none, and 207 could not be opened (152 domains no longer exist). With the emails Overture lists, 522 of 775 businesses had an email. No named people, because no Companies House API key was set.
- **Website company numbers.** Only 17 of 403 Brighton homepages state a company number; 70 mention "Ltd" or "Limited". A cheap later check for businesses we could not match.
- **Not yet measured:** how many directors' names match a published address, how often a guessed address is confirmed, and how many domains accept every address. These need the Companies House API key and a server with port 25 open.
